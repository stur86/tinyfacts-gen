Under the ground where we walk, the world is not still. The hard rock that makes up the top of the world is broken into many big pieces that fit together, and these pieces are always going - very slowly, but all the time.

These big pieces of rock rest on top of very hot, soft stuff that is deep inside the world. The hot stuff goes in slow, big rounds, and as it goes, it pushes the pieces of rock on top of it in different directions. Some pieces go toward each other, some go away from each other, and some go past each other.

Where two pieces meet, they push against each other very hard. The rock along the edges gets pulled and pushed, but it does not go right away. Instead, it builds up more and more force, like a thing that changes how it looks before it breaks. When the force gets too strong for the rock to hold, the rock breaks, and the pieces go quickly.

This is what happens when the ground shakes. The rock breaks along a line deep in the ground, and the push from the break sends waves of shaking through the rock above. These waves go up to the top of the ground, and when they reach the place where people live, the ground goes from side to side or up and down.

The shaking can be very small, so small that only things that are very good at feeling going can pick it up. Or it can be very big, strong enough to break the things people make to live in and make the ground open up. How strong it is is set by how much force was built up and how deep the break is.

After the ground shakes, it can shake again. The break in the rock changes how the force is shared between the pieces, and this can make other breaks happen close to it. Sometimes a big shake is followed by many smaller ones as the rock settles into its new position.

The places where the shaking happens most are where the big pieces of rock come together. Around the edges of these pieces, there are many breaks, and the ground shakes often. In the middle of a piece, the rock is more quiet, and the shaking does not happen much.

Over many hundreds of hundreds of years, the slow going of the pieces has built the world into what we see today. Very high ground has been pushed up where pieces come together. Wide places where the ground goes down have been made where pieces pull away from each other. The world is always changing, even though we do not see it happen in our short lives.
