When you type on a computer and send what you have written to someone far away, the words do not go through the air by themselves. They go through long lines made of glass or hard stuff, or through the air as waves that we do not see. This set of lines and waves joins almost every computer in the world.

Your computer takes what you type and breaks it into very small pieces. Each piece gets a place on it, like a letter, telling where it should go. The pieces then go out of your computer and into a line that goes to other computers, each one passing the pieces along until they reach the place where they were sent.

The pieces do not all go the same way. Each one finds the fastest way it can to get where it is going. Some might go through lines on the ground, some might go through long lines at the bottom of the great wide water, and some might go up high and come back down. When they all get to where they are going, they are put back together in the right order, and the person on the other end can read what you wrote.

The lines that carry these pieces are held up by things all around us. Some go through long glass lines that are smaller than your hair and carry light instead of the red wet stuff. Some go through waves in the air, like the sounds you hear but much faster and too fast for any body to feel.

Not all of these lines are owned by one person or one group. Many different people and groups have built parts of this set, and they all work together so that a piece can go from any place to any other place. When you send something, your piece might go through lines owned by many different groups before it gets to where it is going.

The set also keeps other forms of many things so that people far away can get to them fast. When you look at a picture or read words from far away, your computer is getting those things from another computer far away. But that computer may have gotten them from yet another, and that one from another, like a long line of people passing things from one to the next.

This set works because every computer that is part of it follows the same set of ideas about how to send and get pieces. As long as everyone follows these ideas, a computer in one part of the world can talk to a computer in any other part, and the pieces will find their way there.
