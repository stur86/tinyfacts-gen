Under the ground where we walk, the world is very, very hot. So hot that the hard stuff from deep inside the world turns into thick, bright red wet stuff that can move. This wet stuff is called hot wet rock, and sometimes it pushes its way up through the ground and comes out. When this happens, it is one of the most strong and hard to be close to things on our world.

The ground we walk on is not one piece. It is made of many big pieces that fit together like something you eat from that has been broken. These pieces rest on top of the very hot stuff under them, and they move - very slowly, over many hundreds of years. Where two pieces come close together or pull away from each other, the hot stuff under them can find a way up.

When enough of this hot wet stuff builds up under the ground, it pushes on the rock above it. If the force gets strong enough, the rock breaks and the hot stuff rushes up through the break. This is what happens when a place in the ground opens up. The hot wet rock, along with hot air and small bits of rock, pushes up and out into the sky.

The hot wet rock comes out very hot - hot enough to burn almost anything it touches. It moves down the sides of the high ground where it came out, slowly covering everything in its way. As it moves farther from where it came out, it gets cooler and starts to turn back into hard rock. One on top of another builds up over time, making the ground higher and higher.

The hot air and small rock bits that go up can go very high into the sky. They can block out the sun and make the air hard to breathe. When the small bits come back down, they cover the ground for a long way around. But over many years, this stuff breaks down and makes the ground very good for growing things.

Deep under the biggest body of water on our world, there is more of this hot rock coming up than on all the land put together. Hot stuff comes up from under the water, and when it meets the cold water, it cools fast and forms new hard ground. Over time, this new ground grows high enough to come out of the water, making new land where there was nothing but water before.
