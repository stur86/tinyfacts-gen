When you send a small piece of writing or a sound from one place to another, you are forwarding it. You move it from one person to another until it reaches its finally where it is needed.

Imagine you are sending a piece of paper to a friend. You write what you think on it and then hand it to them. Your friend reads it and then hands it back to you. You are doing this many times, but the paper never changes its form. It keeps being the same piece of paper, even though it is going to many different places.

This is what a computer does with words and pictures. When you send a word or a picture from one computer to another, you are forwarding it. The computer takes what you send, moves it to its own computer, and then sends it back to you. This keeps happening until the word or picture reaches its finally where you need it.

You can also forward things you do not want to keep. If you send an old letter, you are forwarding it. It goes to someone else who wants to read it, or it gets sent to a place where it can be found later. This helps people find what they are looking for, even if they do not know where it started.

In a way, we all forward things all the time. We forward our friends, our work, or our ideas. We send them to others so they can read them or use them.
