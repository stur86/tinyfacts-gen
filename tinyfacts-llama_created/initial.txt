An early thing happens at the beginning of a story, a group of people, or a time. This is the first thing you know about the story. It is the part that sets everything else into place.

Think of it like a room. If you go to a room, you are inside a room. The room is the first thing you see. You do not know what the room looks like until you enter. The room is the beginning. Everything that happens after is built on what you saw before.

In a story, the first thing is often the story that starts it all. If a story starts with a child, the first thing you learn about the story is about the child. Then you learn about the world the child lives in. You learn about the people who care about the child. This is the first part of the story.

When you are very small, you do not know what the world looks like. You do not know how things work. But as you grow, you start to learn. You see the world around you. You learn about the things you can do. You learn how to use your hands and feet. This is the first part of learning.

The first day of school is an early thing. You do not know what the day will bring. You do not know how you will act. You do not know how you will meet your friends. But you are ready. You are ready because you have a plan. You are ready because you know that tomorrow will be different.

Everyone starts somewhere. You start as a small person in a small world. You start as a person who is afraid. You start as a person who does not know what to do. But as you go on, you learn. You learn about the world. You learn about yourself. You learn how to be a person who is ready for the day ahead.
