The word however is a very important word that we use when we want to tell a story that has happened before, but the story still happens now.

When you say this word, you are telling us that what you are about to say happened before something else. You are not saying that the story you are about to tell is still happening. You are saying that what happened before is true.

This word can be very confusing because it seems like it is saying that you are not telling us something. But in fact, you are just telling us that you are talking about a story that already happened. You are building a story from what you know before.

Think of it like this. Imagine you are telling a friend about a time when you were very afraid. You say, however, that it was a good time. It is not a good time now. You are not saying that the time was good. You are just telling your friend that you remember that time.

You can also use this word to ask questions. If you are about to tell a story, and you want to ask, however, you do not want to tell the story, you can say that first. This gives you a chance to think and decide if you really want to continue the story.

So, the word however is a way to talk about what you are about to say. It is a word that helps you build a story from what you already know. It is a word of building stories.
