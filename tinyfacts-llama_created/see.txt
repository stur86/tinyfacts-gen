To see something is to look at it. You look at a thing, a person, a place, or a thing that is not there. Your eyes look at it. Your brain understands what you see.

Think about a tree. If you close your eyes, you are not seeing the tree anymore. The tree is still there, but you do not see it. The tree is only in your brain, and you are only in your body.

If you open your eyes, the tree becomes clear. You see the lines of the wood, the green of the leaves, and the brown of the tree. You see the form of the tree. Your eyes are the ones that see.

Sometimes we see things that are far away. We see a house in the middle of the city. We see a person walking down the street. We see the sun in the sky. We see the stars at night. The sun is in the sky, and we see it.

We also see things that are near us. We see our friends. We see the things we buy every day. We see the food we eat for breakfast, lunch, and dinner. We see the clothes we wear. We see the books we read.

When you see a thing, you are using your brain. Your brain is the part inside your head where all the things you see, hear, and feel come together. Every time you see something, your brain is working to understand what it is.

Sometimes we see things that are not there. You might see a dog running in a field. But the dog is not really there. The dog is only in the picture you see on a computer. The dog is not really a thing. The dog is only a thing that your brain makes out of lines and colors.

Seeing is how we know things are there. It helps us understand the world around us.
