To explain something means to tell people how it is. Imagine you want to tell someone you saw a big dog. You might say, I saw a dog that is very big. Or you might say, The dog is very big. Both of these are different ways to tell the same story.

Sometimes, we need to explain a hard thing. Maybe you are feeling sad because you lost a friend. You might say, Life can be very hard when you lose a person you love. Or you might say, Things are very hard right now. The words help people understand what you mean.

In some cases, we explain a story. If someone tells you a story about a person who did something great, you might ask them to explain why. You might want to know more about the person or why they did what they did. You want to know if the story is true and if it is a good story.

You can also explain a situation with words. If you are walking on a road and you see a car, you might say, There is a car on the road. Or you might say, Cars go on roads. The words help you understand what is happening.

Sometimes, explaining a thing means to show it. If you want to explain why a light is bright, you might turn it on. The light shows you how strong it is. You are telling the world how the light looks.

In short, explaining is about telling others how something is. It is a way to share your ideas or to tell a story.
