To set something down is to write it. You can do this with paper, with your fingers on a box, or even with a tiny thing that holds sound. This makes a story, a picture, a sound, or a voice that you can look at later.

If you are in a place, you might take pictures. You look at the things around you, the walls, the trees, the people. You keep them so you can see them again.

If you are in a room with many people, you might ask them to speak. You write what they say down. This is the same as setting something down, but you are not taking a picture. You are putting words into a book or a computer.

When you set a story down, you are showing the world what happened. It can be a good story, a bad story, or something in between. It helps people know what to expect.

Sometimes, we set things down that are not real. A game is a way to play with a set of words. A song is a set of sounds and words. You might make up a story with these things and people who will listen.

To set something down can also mean to leave it. When you finish a book or a story, you might leave it. You do not have to read it again. The story is now in your memory.
