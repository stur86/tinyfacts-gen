When you tell a group you care about what should happen, you are giving them a chance to decide. In a big group, people sit down and each one has an answer. They might say yes, no, or maybe. The person who has the most say at the end is usually the one who gets to decide.

You can do this in many ways. You can go to a big room with many people, all sitting down in a line. Each person gets a piece of paper and writes their answer. Then, everyone reads all the answers. The person who wrote yes gets a point. The person who wrote no gets a point. The person who wrote maybe gets a point too. At the end, the person with the most points is the one who decides.

Sometimes, people make their answers by doing something. If a group decides that a new park should be built, people might go to a meeting. They talk about it and decide if they like it. If most people say yes, the group decides to build the park.

You can also decide if a person should be in a job. If you tell a group that a person should not be in a job, the group decides what to do. If most people say no, the person might not work there.

Voicing is also about who gets to do a job. In a group, one person might decide who does the work. This can be important, especially if there is no one else to do it. This is why you see people who are in big groups doing things, while others are left to do other jobs.

There are also things you do by giving your answer. If you tell a group that a movie should not be made, people might be afraid to watch it. If you tell a group that a book should not be read, people might feel sad.

So, to summering up, when you tell a group you care about what should happen, you are giving them a chance to decide. You can do this through things you say, things you do, or by telling them who should do a job. Everyone has a say, and it is an important part of how groups work.
