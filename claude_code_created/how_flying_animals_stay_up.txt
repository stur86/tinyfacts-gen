How Flying Animals Stay Up

Everything falls. Drop anything at all and the ground takes it. So how does an animal no heavier than a book stay up in the air for a day at a time, without holding on to anything?

The answer is the air, and the form of the arm it holds out into the air.

Look at that arm from the side. The top is rounded and the bottom is much less so. When the animal moves forward, air has to go both over and under. The air going over has farther to go, and it comes off the back moving down. When you push air down, the air pushes you up. That is all it is. The animal is holding its own body up by throwing air at the ground, hour after hour.

But nothing is free. To make that push you have to keep moving forward through the air, and the air pulls back on you the whole time. So the animal has to work. Its going up and down does two jobs at once: it makes the push up, and it drags the animal forward through the air.

That is why these animals are built the way they are. Almost nothing about them is heavy. The hard parts inside are not full but open, like a room with lines across it, strong for how light they are. They have no teeth. They do not carry their young inside them for months. They eat food that gives a great deal of power for very little in the hand, and they put it to use fast. The very hair on them is not there to keep them warm, or not only. Each piece is a small light cover that catches air and lets none through.

The best of them almost never work at all. Warm air over open ground goes up, and they find those places and go up with them, turning in wide slow rings, almost not moving at all, until they are so high the ground looks like a picture of a place. Then they let go and go down the long slow way toward the next one. Some can cross the great wide water this way and come down on the other side no more tired than when they set out.

Watch one long enough and you will see it stop and hang in the air, on nothing at all, and then drop like a rock on something small that never saw it coming. We have been building things that fly for a hundred years now, and we have still not built one that does that.
