How Green Things Eat Light

Every animal has to eat. People eat, dogs eat, horses eat, and so does every living thing that walks or runs or flies. But a tree does not eat anything at all. A tree makes its own food, out of light.

A tree needs only three things: light from the sun, water from the ground, and one kind of air. Deep inside the green leaves there are tiny cells, and inside those cells sit even tinier green bits. Those green bits are what catch the light.

Using the power in that light, the tree pulls the water and the air to pieces and puts the pieces back together in a new form. What it makes is a sweet stuff, and that sweet stuff is its food. Some of it the tree burns at once for power. Some it builds into more tree: the wood, the leaves, the hard cover on the outside.

There is something left over at the end of all this, and the tree has no use for it, so it lets it go into the air. That left over air is the very air you are breathing right now. Nothing living on land could breathe without it.

So we and the trees hand each other what we each need. We breathe out what they eat. They let go of what we breathe in.

This is also why leaves are green. The green bits take in red light and blue light and use them, but green light they can not use at all, so they throw it back out. The green you see is the light the tree did not want.

When the days grow short and cold, some trees stop the work. The green bits break down, and for a few weeks you can see the reds and browns that were in the leaves all along, hidden under the green. Then the leaves fall, and the tree waits.

Follow any food back far enough and you always end in the same place. The animal ate the green thing. The green thing ate the light. Everything living is living on the sun.
