How Your Voice Gets Across The World

When you speak, you push the air. Something in your throat shakes, the air beside it is pushed and pulled, and that pushing runs out through the room in waves, the way water moves in rings when a rock drops into it. The waves reach the ear of a friend beside you and shake it, and your friend hears you.

But air waves die out fast. Shout as hard as you can and no one on the far side of the city will hear a thing. So people had to find another way to carry the shaking.

Inside the thing you hold up to your face there is a very small cover, so light that the waves from your voice can move it. Behind that cover sits something that turns the moving into power running along a line: a strong push of power when the cover is pushed hard, a small push when it is pushed a little. Your voice has become a run of pushes.

Power will go much farther than sound. It can run along a line of hard stuff under the street, under the great wide water, all the way to the other side of the world. Or it can be thrown out into the air as waves that no one can hear or see, caught by a tall thing on high ground, and passed on again. Light will carry it too. Most of what you say today goes across the world as light, inside long clear lines no thicker than a hair.

And now your voice is first turned into numbers. Very many numbers each second, each one saying how hard the little cover was being pushed at that moment. Numbers are easy to send without losing any of them, and easy to put back together at the other end.

At the far end everything happens the other way round. The numbers become pushes of power. The power shakes a small cover in the ear of your friend. That cover pushes the air. The air pushes on their ear.

So what your friend hears is not your voice at all. It is new air, in a room you have never been in, made to shake in just the way your air shook. It only sounds the same. No one has ever cared.
