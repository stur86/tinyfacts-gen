Pictures Of Places

You know your way home. You could get there with your eyes closed. But if a person from far away asks you how, and you can not walk them there, what do you do? You draw. And when you draw where things are, you have made a picture of a place.

The idea is easy and very old. Look down from above, then put things where they are. What is far away on the ground is far away on the paper. What is off to the right stays off to the right. If it works, the person can take the paper and find their way with no help from you at all.

But making one is harder than it looks. To begin with, the world is round and paper is not. You can not press a round thing down without pulling it out of form somewhere. Every picture of the whole world is wrong in some way. The people who make them get to pick which way it is wrong. Some keep the way from one place to the next right, and let the lands at the top and the bottom grow huge. Some keep the land right and let the ways go strange. There is no picture that has both.

Then there is what to leave out. A picture of a place that showed you everything would be the place, and no use to anyone. So you keep the roads and drop the trees. Or you keep the tall ground and drop the roads. Every one of them is a set of answers to the question: what is this for?

And that is where they stop being only about the world and start being about people. What is set in the middle? Which town gets a name and which does not? Where does the line run between one land and the next, when there is nothing on the ground to see, only two groups who each say the line is somewhere else? Every such picture says as much about who drew it as about what is there.

The strange part is how deep it has gone. Now you can hold up a small bright window and it will tell you where in the world you are standing, to within a few feet, by listening to things going round far over your head. It will draw you the way, and turn as you turn. People still get lost, but not the way they used to.
