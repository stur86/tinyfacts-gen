How People Began To Grow Their Food

For almost the whole story of people, no one grew anything. You walked, you looked, and you took what the ground gave you: green things, small hard bits off the trees, animals if you could catch them. When a place gave no more, you moved on. It was not a bad life. It took only a few hours a day, and there was time left over for other things.

Then, in several different parts of the world, at different times, and with no one telling anyone else, people began to do something new. They began putting the small hard bits into the ground and waiting for what came up.

At first it can only have been a little help on the side. But those who did it noticed something. If you keep the bits from the best growing things, and put those in the ground next year, the next year is better. Do that for a hundred years and you have a growing thing that gives ten times what it gave before, and can no longer live at all without people to put it in the ground.

The same was done with animals. Keep the quiet ones close, let them have young, eat the ones that fight you. In time you had animals that would stay, and give food every day, and pull things too heavy for a person.

This was the turn the whole world came to rest on, but it was not all good. The work was much harder and there was much more of it. The food was of one kind, and people who ate it were smaller and sicker than the ones who had walked and looked. Living close together with so many animals let new ways of getting sick pass over to people for the first time.

And yet it went on, because of one thing: the same land now gave food to many times as many people. You could not go back. There were far too many mouths.

Then came something no walking group had ever had. Food left over. Food that keeps. And once there is food kept in one place, there are people who watch over it, and people who decide who gets it, and people who write down who put in what and who is still to pay. Towns. Then cities. Then everything after.

It all started with someone putting a small hard bit into the ground and coming back to look.
