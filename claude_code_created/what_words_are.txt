What Words Are

Make a sound with your mouth. On its own it means nothing at all. But make it in front of someone who has heard it before, and a picture comes up in their head that was in your head a moment ago. Nothing went across the room but shaking air.

That is the whole of it, and no one knows how it started.

There is nothing in the sound of a word that ties it to the thing. Nothing about a dog makes the sound "dog" the right one. Other people in other places make a completely different sound and get the same picture. The tie is not in the sound. The tie is in the heads of everyone who agreed, long before you came into the world, without ever talking it over.

What makes it more than a set of names is the order. Take the same few words and put them in a different order and you get something new: the dog saw the man, the man saw the dog. No one had to be told either of those before to know which is which. And that means you can say something that has never once been said by anyone, and be understood the first time, by someone who has never heard it. No other animal does this.

You did not learn any of this. No one sat you down. Before you were four years old you had worked out, only by listening, most of the order a full grown person uses, and you did it while you were still eating off the floor.

Words also do more than point. They can carry what is not here: what happened before you came into the world, what has not happened yet, what could never happen at all. A person can hand you a picture of a place they have only heard about, and you will hold it for the rest of your life.

And so nothing anyone works out has to be worked out again. Every one of us builds on top of what dead people said. That, more than our hands or our brains, is why we are the way we are.
