What Money Is

Say you build chairs, and you want food. You have to find someone who has food, who also wants a chair, and who wants it today. Most days you will not find that person. And even if you do, one chair is a great deal of food. More than you could carry home, and more than you could eat before it went bad.

Money was made to get around this. It is a way of holding on to what you have done for other people until the day you need something back from them.

You give the chair to the person who wants a chair, and they hand you money. The money is not food. You can not sit on it. On its own it does nothing for you at all. But everyone has agreed to take it. So you can carry it home, keep it for a year, and then hand it to someone who has food and does not want a chair.

For a long time money was small round pieces of a hard bright stuff taken out of the ground. People said the stuff was good in its own right, and there was only so much of it in the world. Later it became paper, and the paper stood for the bright stuff, kept somewhere safe. Now most of it is not even paper. It is only numbers held in computers, moved from one name to another and back again.

At every step the same question comes up. Why should anyone take this? And the answer has always been the same one. Because they believe the next person will take it too.

That is the whole of it. Money is not really a thing. It is what a great many people believe about a thing, all at the same time. When enough of them stop believing, the money is paper again, whatever is written on it. This has happened, more than once, to people who were sure it could not.
