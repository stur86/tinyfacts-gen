The Things Everyone Must Follow

Put two people in a room and they will work out between them how to act. If one takes too much, the other says so, and it gets settled. People lived this way for a very long time, in small groups where everyone knew everyone else and had to see them again tomorrow.

Now put a hundred hundred people in one city. Most of them will never meet. There is no one to say so, and nothing to settle it with. So people write down what everyone has to do and what no one may do, and then they hold every person to it, whether that person ever agreed to it or not.

Three things have to be true, or it does not work at all.

The first is that it must be the same for everyone. If some may take and others may not, then it is not something everyone follows. It is only the strong doing what they want, with the words written down after.

The second is that people must be able to know it before they act. You can not be told when it is over that what you did was not allowed. If no one can know what will be allowed tomorrow, no one can plan anything at all.

The third is that it must hold the people who wrote it as tight as it holds everyone else. If those who write it do not have to follow it, then they answer to nothing, and everyone else answers to them.

When someone is said to have gone against it, we do not let the person who is angry decide. We ask others, who were not there and who get nothing either way, to listen to both sides and then say what they think happened. They can get it wrong. Sometimes they do get it wrong, and someone pays for it who did nothing. It is still better than the other way, where the one with the most friends is the one who was right.

None of this is strong on its own. It is words on paper. It works because almost everyone follows it almost all the time without being made to, and that lasts only as long as people believe it is the same for them as for everyone else.
