Why The Year Turns Warm And Cold

Every year the same thing happens. The days grow longer and warmer until you can stay outside at night. Then they grow shorter and colder until the trees are empty and the water at your door is hard.

Almost everyone believes they know why, and almost everyone has it wrong. They think the world must be closer to the sun in the warm part of the year. It is not. It is closest in the middle of the cold part, and how much closer is far too little to matter anyway.

Here is what is really going on. The world turns round and round, and the line it turns about does not stand straight up. It leans over to one side. And here is the important part: it goes on leaning the same way all year. As the world goes round the sun, that lean stays pointing at the same far off star. So for half the year the top of the world is leaning toward the sun, and for the other half it is leaning away.

When your half is leaning toward the sun, two things happen at once. The sun comes up higher over your head, so its light hits the ground more straight on and the same light is not put out over so much ground. And the sun is up for more hours, so it has more time to make things warm. Warm days, and then warm nights, and it grows warmer week after week.

Half a year later your half leans away. The sun stays low, its light comes in from the side, and the night is longer than the day. The ground gives up what warm it has to the sky, and not enough is coming in to put it back.

Which is why the year is the other way round at the bottom of the world. When one half is warm the other half is cold, and it has always been so. There are people who have the hottest day of the year in the middle of what you call the cold months, and it does not feel strange to them at all.

Go far enough toward the top and the lean does something even harder to believe. For weeks in the warm part of the year the sun goes round and round the sky and never goes down at all, and in the cold part it never comes up. People there live half a year in day and half in night.
