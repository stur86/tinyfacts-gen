Things That Tell Us The Hour

For most of the time there have been people, no one knew what hour it was. The sun came up, went over, and went down, and that was as much as anyone needed to know.

To cut a day into parts you need something that does the same thing over and over, and takes just as long every time.

The first answer was the sun. Stand a straight stick in the ground and watch its shadow turn through the day. It works, but not at night, and not under the grey stuff hanging in the sky.

Then people used things running out. Water going out of a small opening in the bottom of a cup. Dry bits falling from one glass room into another through a middle so tight that only a few can pass at once. Turn it over and it starts again. But someone has to be there to turn it.

The great step was finding something that will not be hurried. Hang something heavy on a line and push it. It goes across and comes back, across and back. Push it harder and it goes farther, but it takes just as long to get there and back. It does not care how hard you push. Give it one small push each time it comes by, and it will keep going all day.

Now put teeth around the edge of a round piece of hard stuff. Let the heavy hanging thing let those teeth by, one at a time, one for each time it goes across. Have something slowly falling turn the round piece, so it is always pressing on. Now that round piece can move on by only one of those teeth at a time. Put a hand on the front of it, and there is your hour.

Later, people did the same with a piece of hard stuff wound up tight, which shakes this way and that in place instead of hanging. That would go in a pocket, and it did not care whether you were on a horse or out on the great wide water.

Today the shaking thing is a tiny bit of rock cut to just the right form. Push a little power into it and it shakes many hundreds of hundreds of times each second, each shake taking just as long as the last. Something that small keeps the hour better than anything with teeth ever did.

And the best of them now stand in cold rooms, watching the shaking of the tiny pieces inside one kind of stuff. If one of those had been set going before there were any people at all, it would still not be off by a single second.
