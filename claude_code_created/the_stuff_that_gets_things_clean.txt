The Stuff That Gets Things Clean

Try this. Get the thick soft stuff that comes off hot food on to your hands, then hold your hands under running water. The water runs off. The thick stuff stays. You can rub as hard as you like and most of it will still be there when you are done.

The reason is that water and that thick stuff want nothing to do with each other. Water is made of tiny pieces that pull on one another very hard, and they would much rather hold on to each other than to anything else in the world. The thick stuff is made of pieces that do not pull that way at all. Put the two together and the water pulls in on all sides into balls and pushes the other out.

Now use the stuff we keep beside the water for getting clean.

Every tiny piece of it has two ends, and the two ends want different things. One end loves water and will not let go of it. The other end can not stand water, and would much rather be sitting in the thick soft stuff.

Put it on your hands under running water, and every one of those tiny pieces turns the same way round. The ends that hate water push into the thick stuff. The ends that love water stay out. Before long, each bit of the thick stuff is closed up inside a tiny ball, with all the water loving ends pointing out. To the water, that ball now looks like something it likes. So the water carries the ball away, and everything closed up inside it goes too.

This is also why it kills so many of the tiny living things that make people sick. A great many of them are held together by a cover made of much the same soft stuff. The very same ends that pull that stuff to pieces pull the cover to pieces, and the living thing falls open and dies.

None of this happens the moment you touch the water. The tiny pieces need a little time to turn round and take hold. That is why you are told to keep rubbing for as long as it takes to say a short song two times over.
