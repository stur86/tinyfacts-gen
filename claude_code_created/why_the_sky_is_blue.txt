Why The Sky Is Blue

Look up on a clear day. The sky is blue from one edge of the world to the other. But the air over your head has no color in it at all. You can see through it for a very long way.

So where does the blue come from?

The light that comes from the sun looks white, but white light is really every color at once, all put together. Each color moves through the air as waves, and the waves are not the same for each one. Red light moves in long slow waves. Blue light moves in short quick ones.

The air is full of tiny bits of stuff, far too small to see. When light runs into one of these bits, some of it can be thrown off to the side instead of going straight on. Short quick waves are thrown off much more easily than long slow ones. Blue is thrown off most of all.

So blue light does not come straight down to you from the sun. It is thrown this way and that, from bit to bit, all through the air over your head, until at last some of it falls into your eye. It reaches you from every part of the sky at once. That is why the whole sky is blue, and not only the small spot where the sun sits.

Now watch the end of the day. The sun is low, and its light has to run through much more air to reach you than it did in the middle of the day. Along that long way, almost all the blue is thrown off before it ever gets to you. What is left is the long slow waves, and so the sun and the sky around it turn red.

Out in space there is no air. Nothing is there to throw the light about. So the sky is black, even with the sun burning right in front of you. People who have been up there say that is the strangest part of all. The sun is out, and it is still night.
