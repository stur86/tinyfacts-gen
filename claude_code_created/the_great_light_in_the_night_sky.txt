The Great Light In The Night Sky

Some nights there is a full white round face hanging over the world. Two weeks later there is only a small bright edge. And some nights there is nothing there at all. People have been watching this change and asking why for as long as there have been people.

It is a huge dead rock, and it makes no light of its own. Everything you see of it is light from the sun falling on its side.

The sun can light up only one half of that rock at a time, the half turned toward the sun. The other half stays dark. The rock goes round and round the world, taking about four weeks each time, and as it moves we look at that lit half from a different direction every night.

When it sits far away from the sun in our sky, we are looking straight at the lit half, and we see a full round white face. When it comes round to sit close to the sun, we are looking at the dark half, and there is almost nothing to see. Between those two we get part light and part dark: a half face, or a small bright edge.

Nothing is covering it. Nothing is in the way. We are only seeing more or less of a side that is always half lit.

This is where our months come from. Long before anyone could write, people were cutting marks into wood, one for each night, and finding that the same face came back after the same number of marks. It was the first way anyone had of holding on to time.

Here is the strangest part. The same face is always turned toward us. It turns round just once in the time it takes to go round the world, so one whole side of it had never been seen by anyone who has ever lived, until we sent something up and round the back to look.

And it pulls on us. That pull lifts the great wide water toward it, and the water climbs up the land and falls back again, two times every day, in every part of the world, and it has never once stopped.
