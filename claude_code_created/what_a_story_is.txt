What A Story Is

Every group of people who has ever lived has told stories. Not some of them. All of them. We do it around a fire, we do it to children who will not sleep, we do it for hours in dark rooms in front of a great bright wall. Anything that every person in the world does is something to look at hard.

Here is the strange part. Almost none of it is true, and we know it is not true, and we want it anyway.

A story is not a run of things that happened. Things happen to you all day and none of it is a story. A story is a small number of things put in an order that makes each one come out of the one before. This happened, and because of it that happened, and because of that, this. Take the "because" out and you have only a long day.

There is nearly always someone who wants something and can not have it. That is what makes the whole thing go. If they get it at once there is no story. Something has to stand in the way: another person, the world, or, best of all, some part of them. And that want has to matter enough that we care whether they get it, which means we have to know them a little first.

Then the thing turns. What they wanted turns out not to be the thing they needed, or it takes more from them than they thought, or they get it and are not made happy. That turn is the part people remember for the rest of their lives.

Why do we want this? Look at what it does. You sit still in one place and you live through something that never happened to you. You feel what it is like to be old, or to lose a child, or to have to pick between two people you love. When the day comes that something like it happens to you for real, some small part of you has been there before. It is the only way anyone has ever found to hand a life over to someone else.

That is also why the stories that stay are not the ones where everything is fine. No one keeps those. We keep the hard ones, and we hand them on, because what we are really handing each other is what it takes to be a person.
